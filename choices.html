Every choice, and what it tends to cost

"Faisla" means decision. This page does not tell you which one to make. For 26 common choices about marriage, children, work, home and honesty, it sets out what people tend to get, what it tends to cost, who else pays, and what nobody knows.

Part 1 · Instructions

Read this first

Tags mean the same as on Form A: Survey a published source, Seen often familiar but unmeasured, Not sure we don't know.

  1. No option is free

    Every choice below has a bill. Not choosing is also a choice, and its bill arrives later and is usually larger.

  2. The label matters less than the people

    Studies that compare love and arranged marriages do not agree on a winner. One study of Indian couples in the US found no difference in love, satisfaction or commitment. A 2025 review of Indian research found women in arranged marriages often report lower satisfaction, with education and city life changing the picture. Survey So the type of marriage tells you little. The two people and the two families tell you a lot.

  3. The same choice has different prices

    For a woman, for someone without income, for someone from a caste or religion that is treated as lower, and for anyone in a small place where everyone knows everyone, almost every bill on this page is higher. Seen often

  4. You pay alone. Do not decide alone.

    In the end the life is yours and so is the cost. Before that, talk to someone who has actually lived the choice, not someone with an opinion about it.

  5. "Tends to" means tends to

    Almost nothing below is measured. These are patterns people commonly report. Your case can go the other way. Not sure

Part 7 · Self-declaration

Seven questions before you decide

  1. Can I pay for this choice myself? In money, and in months of silence.
  2. Who else pays? Have I told them, or am I deciding for them?
  3. What is my floor if it fails? Where do I sleep, and who picks up the phone?
  4. Am I choosing this, or running from something else?
  5. Which regret can I live with? There will be one either way.
  6. Would I still choose it if nobody ever found out? If not, it may be a status move. See Form B.
  7. Have I spoken to one person who has lived it?

If the choice is being made for you by force

Being hit, locked in, threatened, having your documents taken, or being forced into a marriage is harm, not a family disagreement. Tell someone outside the house and call for help.

Part 8 · Declaration of ignorance

What this page does not know

  • Outcomes. For almost every choice here, nobody has followed Indian families for years and counted how it went. We are describing, not predicting.
  • Which marriage type is happier. The research is small, mixed, and mostly urban.
  • How often families come round after a marriage they opposed.
  • How common violence over marriage choices is. It is under-reported, and risk differs sharply by place and community.
  • The law in your state. Rules on live-in relationships and inter-faith marriage differ by state and are changing. We are not lawyers.
  • Medical questions, such as fertility and age. Ask a doctor.
  • Regret. We found no Indian data on who regrets which choice.
  • Queer and trans lives, where several of these choices are not legally available at all.

Part 9 · Closing

Whichever you choose

You will make these choices with half the information, under pressure, at an age when you barely know yourself. So did your parents. Whatever you pick, some of it will go wrong, and it will still have been yours. Nobody else can live it for you, and nobody can take the bill. What they can do is sit with you while you pay it. Choose with those people in mind, and do not keep count of what they owe you for staying.
