Application for permission to live your own life

Permission is not actually required. But nobody tells you that, and nobody hands you the rulebook either. This is the rulebook: what India expects from you at each age, why it expects it, how it changes with where you live, and what you can do about it without fighting anyone.

Part 1 · Instructions, read before filling

How the whole thing works

Six rules. Once you see them, most of the pressure stops feeling personal.

  1. Every expectation is a fear wearing formal clothes

    India has thin pensions, patchy health cover and no unemployment cheque. So the family is the insurance company, and you are the policy. One study of young men in Indian cities found that money spent on a boy is treated as cover for his parents' old age. When someone says "settle down", they are usually saying "please be safe, so that I can be safe."

  2. The timetable is a hand-me-down

    Degree by 22, job by 23, marriage by 27, child by 30, flat by 32. This schedule was written for an economy where one salary bought a house and jobs lasted forty years. Your parents followed it because it worked for them. They are giving you the best map they have. The roads have moved.

  3. "Log" are about four people

    Ask anyone to name the people whose opinion they fear. The list is short: one bua, one neighbour, two relatives from the wedding circuit. Tamil even counts them. The phrase is literally "what will four people say". Four people is a small committee to hand your life to.

  4. Your parents are being graded too

    Your marks, your package and your wedding are their report card in front of their own four people. The aunty asking about your marriage was asked the same question in 1994 by an aunty who was asked in 1968. It is a relay race of worry, and nobody remembers who started it.

  5. Money talks in a voice everyone understands

    In most Indian homes, the person who pays has the bigger vote. This is unfair to say and useful to know. Your own income, even a small one, changes how your sentences land at the dinner table.

  6. You are also "log"

    For some younger cousin, some junior, some friend who quit their job, you are one of the four people. This is the best news in the form. If you are part of the pressure, you can also be the part that stops.

Section A · Age 15 to 18

The marks years

You have the least power you will ever have and the most people checking on you. Both are temporary.

A1 · Standard questions
  • "Kitne percent aaye?"
  • "Science liya na?"
  • "Phone rakh do. Sharma ji ka beta dekho."
  • "Ek baar entrance nikal jaye, phir life set hai."
A2 · What is going on underneath

For most Indian families, education is the only lift that goes up. Your parents are not in love with marks. They are terrified of you getting stuck, and marks are the only dial they know how to read. Nobody told them there are other dials.

Appearance starts getting commented on now too. In a national youth survey, more than half of young people said they worry about how they look. You are not vain. You are being watched.

A3 · Where you live changes it
MetroCoaching from class 8. You are compared with the whole apartment WhatsApp group. More options, more comparison.
Small cityThree approved careers: engineer, doctor, sarkari. The coaching hub in another city is sold as the only ticket out.
VillageStaying in school can itself be the fight, more so for girls. Housework and farm work compete with homework, and marriage talk can start early. A large survey found seven in ten teenage girls want to finish graduation. The wish is there. The road is what's missing.
Abroad"We moved here for you." The sacrifice arrives as an invoice, payable in grades.
A4 · Moves
  1. Report before you are asked. Parents panic at silence. A five-minute weekly update buys more freedom than any argument.
  2. Bring a plan, not a mood. Want arts, design, sports? Show the course, the college, the jobs and the backup. A plan gets a hearing. "I just don't like science" gets a lecture.
  3. Borrow an adult. A teacher, older cousin or mama can say your sentence in a voice your parents trust.
  4. One mark sheet is weather. Your life is climate. On the day it feels like everything, tell one person. That day passes faster when it is shared.
Line to keep in your pocket

"Sharma ji ka beta bhi kisi aur ke Sharma ji ke bete se compare ho raha hai."

Sharma ji's son is also being compared with somebody else's Sharma ji's son.

Section B · Age 19 to 24

The package years

The question changes from "how much did you score" to "how much do you earn". Same question, new unit.

B1 · Standard questions
  • "Placement hua? Kitna package?"
  • "Government job ki taiyari kar lo. Safe hai."
  • "Itni door kyun jaana hai?" (asked far more often of daughters)
  • "Padhai ho gayi. Ab rishte dekhein?" (in many homes, to women, the week after the last exam)
B2 · What is going on underneath

When young Indians were asked what worries them most, the top answer was their family's financial security, ahead of their own health. Jobs came right after. Your first salary is not only yours in the family's mind. It is the safety net finally arriving.

Meanwhile you are comparing yourself with LinkedIn, which is the aunty network with better fonts.

B3 · Where you live changes it
MetroThe hustle script: startup, side gig, "what are you building?" Rent eats the salary, and loneliness is the bill nobody mentions.
Small citySarkari naukri is the gold standard, so years go into attempts. Relationships stay secret because everybody knows everybody.
VillageEarn now. Many young men migrate for work. For many young women, the wedding arrives before the degree does.
AbroadLoans, visas, and two opposite instructions from home: "come back soon" and "don't you dare come back".
B4 · Moves
  1. Earn something early. Tuitions, freelancing, a part-time job. Even ₹5,000 a month of your own changes your standing at home.
  2. Put a date on your gamble. "Two UPSC attempts, till June 2028. After that, plan B is this." Families can live with a deadline. They cannot live with fog.
  3. Share your life in small doses. Mention your friends, your city, your doubts. Surprise is what turns a choice into a scandal.
  4. Compare with yourself from last year. Everyone else's reel is edited. So is yours.
Line to keep in your pocket

"Package chhota hai, par EMI bhi nahi hai. Hisaab se main ameer hoon."

The package is small, but so is my EMI. On paper, I'm rich.

Section C · Age 25 to 29

The shaadi years

Every wedding you attend becomes a public hearing on your own.

C1 · Standard questions
  • "Shaadi kab kar rahe ho? Koi hai kya?"
  • "Umar nikal rahi hai." (women hear it earlier and louder)
  • "Settle ho gaye? Ghar liya?" (men hear this one)
  • "Apni community mein hi dekhna."
C2 · What is going on underneath

A marriage here is a merger of two families, so everybody feels like a shareholder. In a national survey, 84% of married young people said their parents arranged it. And here is the gap that explains a lot of quiet heartbreak: about six in ten young Indians say they support inter-caste marriage, while only about one marriage in twenty actually is one.

Also, you are not late. The share of 15 to 34 year olds who are married has dropped 13 points since 2007. The whole country is moving the date. Your relatives have not received the memo.

C3 · Where you live changes it
MetroA dating app and a biodata, both running at once. Living alone is questioned less. The wedding costs more.
Small cityThe clock rings at 24 or 25 for women. A partner from another caste or religion is the hardest news to carry home.
VillageMany are already married. The questions have moved on to children, earning, and whether a wife may work.
Abroad"Find someone from our community." Trips home turn into rishta camps with jet lag.
C4 · Moves
  1. Know your own answer first. Yes, no, not now, or yes but my way. Vague answers invite follow-up questions.
  2. If it is "not now", name a month. "Ask me again after March 2028." Then actually have the conversation in March 2028.
  3. If your partner is not who they expect, go in order. Income first. A place to stay. One ally in the family. Then one parent, then both. The sequence matters more than the speech. Two adults can legally marry across caste or religion in India under the Special Marriage Act.
  4. If the script has no page for you, because you are queer or do not want marriage at all, find your people before you make any announcements. Telling can wait until you are safe.
Line to keep in your pocket

"Aunty, jab hogi, pehla card aapka. Tab tak aap paneer lijiye."

Aunty, when it happens, the first card is yours. Until then, please have some paneer.

Section D · Age 30 to 34

The "ab toh" years

"Ab toh kar lo." At least do it now. The sentence fits everything: marriage, baby, flat, second baby.

D1 · Standard questions
  • "Good news kab de rahe ho?"
  • "Ghar kab le rahe ho? Kiraye mein paisa barbaad hai."
  • "Mummy-papa ko paas bula lo." Or: "Wapas aa jao."
  • If single: "Sab theek toh hai na?" with a tilted head.
D2 · What is going on underneath

The roles are flipping. Your parents are ageing and you are becoming the safety net. About four in ten Indians say sons should carry the main duty of caring for ageing parents. Only 2% say daughters should. So sons feel a weight nobody says aloud, and daughters do the caring anyway without the credit.

On children, a 2026 UN survey found young Indians are delaying family mostly because of money worries and unequal housework, not because they have stopped wanting one. It is arithmetic. People keep calling it selfishness.

D3 · Where you live changes it
MetroEMI, school fees, two jobs. Childcare still lands mostly on her. You are the filling in a sandwich between your kids and your parents.
Small cityJoint family negotiations: whose kitchen, whose rules, whose turn to host.
VillageYou are already a senior. The talk is about sons, land, and who has to leave for work.
AbroadParents growing old 8,000 km away. Guilt, delivered across two time zones.
D4 · Moves
  1. Have the money talk with your parents. What they have, what they will need, health insurance. One awkward evening saves ten anxious years.
  2. On children, two people have a vote. Practise saying that warmly. Warm and firm is a skill, and it improves with use.
  3. If you are single at 32, you are not a pending file. Furnish the house. Build the friendships. Book the health check-up. Do not live in a waiting room.
  4. Stop the chain going down. Do not ask your 22-year-old cousin about her package. Ask what she is enjoying.
Line to keep in your pocket

"Good news yeh hai ki hum khush hain. Baaki bulletin baad mein."

The good news is that we're happy. The rest of the bulletin comes later.

Part 3 · Works at any age

The no-fight toolkit

None of this needs shouting, slammed doors or a dramatic exit. Drama is expensive. These are cheap.

T1 · Sort everything into three piles

Not up for vote

Your safety, your body, who you marry, whether you have children, your basic dignity.

Open to a deal

Timing, city, how big the wedding is, how often you visit, which rituals you keep.

Let them win

The kurta at Diwali. The photo with the whole family. The second helping. Small wins for them buy big room for you.

T2 · Six habits
  1. Answer the fear, skip the question. "Shaadi kab?" means "will you be alone?" Reply to that one.
  2. Agree with the feeling, keep your plan. "You're right to worry. Here is what I'm doing about it."
  3. Talk in private. Nobody changes their mind in front of an audience. Correct an elder in public and you have created a second problem.
  4. Find one ally. Every family has one reasonable mausi or chacha. Convince one person and let them convince the rest.
  5. Use humour as a door. A joke lets everybody leave the topic with their respect intact. Aim it at the situation, never at the person.
  6. Repeat calmly. The same kind sentence, said the fifth time in the same tone, works better than a new argument each time.

When it stops being pressure

Everything above is for nagging, comparison and guilt. It is not for being hit, locked in, threatened, having your documents taken, or being forced into a marriage. That is harm, and humour is the wrong tool. Tell someone outside the house and call for help. If the pressure has brought you to thoughts of ending your life, please call too. People pick up.

Part 4 · The controversial page

Things nobody says out loud

Some of these will annoy your parents. Some will annoy you. That is how you know the list is fair.

  1. Your parents are right about some of it

    Savings, health, showing up for family, not burning bridges. Old advice is not automatically a cage. Keep what works.

  2. Rebellion can be a script too

    Quit the job, travel, start up, post about it. If you are doing it because your feed expects it, you have only changed which four people you obey.

  3. Freedom without income is a debate

    You can win every argument at home and still need to ask for rent. Build the money first. Then the conversation is between equals.

  4. We are more modern in surveys than in weddings

    Most young Indians say caste should not matter in marriage. Almost all of us still marry inside it. Nobody is lying. Saying it costs nothing, and doing it can cost you your family. Be gentle with people stuck in that gap, yourself included.

  5. Sons and daughters are squeezed by the same machine

    She is told what she may not do. He is told what he must provide. In one large survey, nearly nine in ten Indians agreed a wife must always obey her husband, and women agreed almost as often as men. It is not men against women. It is everybody against an old rulebook.

  6. Where you were born sets the difficulty level

    In that same survey, about a third of people in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka fully agreed with the "obey" line. In Uttar Pradesh and Bihar it was about four in five. Same country, different game settings. Do not judge someone's choices until you know what level they are playing on.

  7. There is no villain

    The aunty, the uncle, your father, you. Everyone is doing what was done to them, mostly out of love and fear. That is why this can end without anybody losing.
