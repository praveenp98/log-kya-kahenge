Application for permission to live your own life

Permission is not actually required. Nobody tells you that, and nobody hands you the rulebook either. This is an attempt at the rulebook: what India expects of you between 15 and 34, why, how it changes with where you live, and what you can do without violence. It will not make anyone agree with you. It may make you less confused.

Part 0 · Read before filling

How honest this form is

Every claim on this page carries one of three tags, so you know how much to trust it.

SurveyA published survey or official record says so. The source is in the Numbers section. Seen oftenWidely reported and familiar to most Indians, but we have no number for it. It may not be true of your family. Not sureWe don't know. It is a guess, or the evidence is thin, old or missing.
What this form cannot do
  • It cannot give you a sentence that makes your family change. No such sentence exists.
  • It cannot tell you what to choose. It can only show you what each choice tends to cost.
  • It was drafted with the help of an AI system. No sociologist, lawyer or counsellor has checked it.
  • Most of the surveys behind it lean urban and are a few years old. Read the Don't know section before you quote it to anyone.

Part 1 · Instructions

How the whole thing works

Eight rules. The first six explain the pressure. The last two are the ones nobody likes hearing.

  1. Most expectations are fear in formal clothes

    India has thin pensions, patchy health cover and no unemployment cheque. So the family is the insurance company and you are the policy. A study of young men in Indian cities found that money spent on a boy is treated as cover for his parents' old age. Survey "Settle down" often means "be safe, so that I can be safe." Not always. Sometimes it is control, or habit, or status. Not sure how often it is which.

  2. The timetable is a hand-me-down

    Degree by 22, job by 23, marriage by 27, child by 30, flat by 32. The schedule was written for an economy where one salary bought a house. In 2022, the unemployment rate among young graduates was 29%, against 3% for young people who cannot read. Survey Your parents are giving you the best map they have. The roads have moved.

  3. "Log" are about four people

    Ask anyone to name the people whose opinion they fear. The list is short. Tamil even counts them: the phrase is "what will four people say". Seen often

  4. Your parents are being graded too

    Your marks, package and wedding are their report card before their own four people. The aunty asking about your marriage was asked the same thing decades ago. Seen often

  5. Money has the loudest vote

    In most homes, the person who pays decides more. That is unfair to people who cannot earn yet, and to women who are kept from earning. It is still how it works. Seen often

  6. You are also "log"

    For some younger cousin or junior, you are one of the four. You are part of the pressure, so you can be part of where it stops.

  7. No sentence will fix it

    Films end with a speech and a hug. Homes mostly don't. People change slowly, through years of watching you live, or they don't change. Clever replies end a conversation. They rarely change a mind. Seen often

  8. You arrive alone and you leave alone

    Nobody can sit your exam for you, live inside your marriage for you, or take your place at the end. So the last decision about your life is yours, and so is the bill. That same fact is why the people who walk beside you matter so much. And it is why they cannot be measured in marks, salary, obedience or sacrifice. The moment you start counting, it stops being a relationship and becomes an account.

Part 2 · Schedule of expectations

The full list

Eleven areas, as complete as we could make it. Not every item applies to every family, and the list is certainly missing things.

L1 · Studies and marks
  • Top marks, every time. Marks are read as character, not as one exam on one day. Seen often
  • Science after class 10. Commerce is the compromise. Arts is treated as what is left over. Seen often
  • Engineer, doctor or government officer. Everything else needs explaining. Seen often
  • Coaching, often in another city, from 14 or 15. Seen often
  • Good English. Treated as proof of intelligence. Seen often
  • No gaps. A year off is allowed only to retake an entrance exam. Seen often
  • For many girls, school ends when marriage, money or distance gets in the way. One in five women aged 20 to 24 was married before 18. In villages it is 23%, in cities 11%. Survey
  • The cost. 14,488 students died by suicide in 2024, up about 80% in ten years. Survey How much of that is exam pressure, we do not know. Police records name exam failure in only a small share, and those records are rough. Not sure
L2 · Work and money
  • A job straight after the degree. A gap is read as failure. Yet young people are about 83% of India's unemployed. Survey
  • A government job is the only real job. Seen often
  • Your salary is public information. Seen often
  • Sons provide. For parents, for a sister's wedding, for a house. 43% of Indians say earning is mainly the man's job. Survey
  • Daughters may work, within limits. A "suitable" job, near home, and not above the husband. About 8 in 10 Indians agree men should get jobs first when jobs are scarce. Only 25% of women aged 15 to 59 are in employment, against 75% of men. Survey
  • Send money home. Seen often
  • Buy a house. Rent is "waste". Seen often
  • Take no risks. No startup, art, sport or career switch, unless you have already succeeded at it. Seen often
  • Join the family business, shop or farm. Seen often
  • Go abroad, or never go abroad. Families differ sharply and we have no data on which is more common. Not sure
L3 · Marriage
  • You will marry. Staying single is not offered as an option. Seen often
  • By a deadline. Roughly mid-twenties for women, late twenties for men, earlier in villages. The exact age differs by family and place and we have no reliable figure for it. Not sure
  • Parents choose or approve. 84% of married young people said their marriage was arranged by parents. Survey
  • Same caste, same religion. Often same language and region too. About 5% of marriages are inter-caste. Survey
  • Dowry. Illegal since 1961, and still expected in many marriages, often renamed "gifts". We could not find a recent national number we trust. Not sure
  • A big wedding. Paid for with savings or loans, mostly by the bride's side. Seen often
  • She moves, she adjusts. The wife joins the husband's home and learns its rules. Seen often
  • The biodata checklist. Horoscope, skin colour, height, salary, "homely". Seen often
  • Stay married. Divorce is read as failure. Acceptance is higher among educated and urban young people. Survey
  • If you are queer, marry the opposite sex anyway. Seen often As far as we could check in November 2026, Indian law still does not recognise same-sex marriage. Survey
L4 · Children
  • A child within a year or two of the wedding. Seen often
  • At least one son. 63% of Indians say sons, not daughters, should mainly handle parents' last rites. Survey
  • Two children. One is "lonely", none is "selfish". About four in ten women and a third of men name two as ideal. Survey
  • The mother slows down. The father does not. Women spend 289 minutes a day on unpaid housework, men 88. On caregiving, 137 minutes against 75. Survey
  • Grandparents get a vote on how the child is raised. Seen often
  • If there is no child, she is blamed first. Seen often
  • Choosing to have no children. We do not know how common it is in India, or how families respond over time. Not sure
L5 · Body and looks
  • Fair skin. Pressed hardest on women. Seen often
  • The right weight. Comments on your body work as a greeting. More than half of young Indians say they worry about their appearance. Survey
  • Men must be tall, strong and not bald. Body-shaming among boys is documented in city studies. Survey
  • Dress "decently". The rule tightens for girls at puberty and again after marriage. Seen often
  • Hair, beard, tattoos and piercings are read as character. Seen often
  • Periods are not discussed. In some homes they bring kitchen or prayer restrictions. This differs widely by family and community. Not sure
L6 · Freedom and izzat
  • Home before dark. Mostly for daughters. Seen often
  • The family's honour lives in a daughter's behaviour. A son's behaviour is "boys will be boys". Seen often
  • No dating. Or dating, in secret. Seen often
  • The "right" friends. Young men are more likely than young women to weigh caste, money and English when making friends. Survey
  • Your phone can be checked. More often for girls. We have no number for how common this is. Not sure
  • Do not live alone before marriage. Suspicious for anyone, more so for women. Seen often
  • Do not drink or smoke. Or do not be seen. Seen often
  • Never answer back to an elder. Seen often
L7 · Caste, religion and community
  • Marry inside. Six in ten young people say they support inter-caste marriage. One marriage in twenty is one. Survey
  • Eat inside. Food rules mark who belongs. In 2011-12, 30% of rural and 20% of urban households said they practised untouchability. That figure is old. Survey
  • Keep the fasts, festivals and prayers. Seen often
  • Rent only where you are allowed. Landlords screening by religion, caste, food and marital status is widely reported. We have no number. Seen often
  • Your surname speaks before you do. Seen often
  • If your group faces discrimination: be twice as good and do not complain. Many people report this. We cannot speak for it. Not sure
  • What we are missing. How these pressures differ for Dalit, Adivasi, Muslim, Christian, Sikh and north-eastern young people. This page does not know enough to say. Not sure
L8 · Roles inside the home
  • A wife obeys. 87% of Indian adults agree a wife must always obey her husband. Women agree almost as often as men. Survey
  • She runs the house, even if she also earns. Survey
  • Sons look after parents. About four in ten say sons should carry the main duty. 2% say daughters. Survey
  • Men do not cry or ask for help. Seen often
  • A daughter-in-law serves. A son-in-law is served. Seen often
  • Brothers guard sisters. Seen often
  • If you are trans or non-binary, the expectation is that you do not exist. This page knows too little here. Not sure
L9 · Language, class and status
  • English means smart. Accent means class. Seen often
  • Speak the "right" language for the room. Your mother tongue can be mocked in a metro and demanded at home. Seen often
  • "Good family" and "well settled". Often code for money and caste. Seen often
  • First in the family to reach college. You carry everyone's hope and nobody has a map for you. Seen often
  • The scoreboard. Phone, bike, car, flat, wedding. Seen often
L10 · Parents and elders
  • Live with them or near them. Expected mainly of sons. Survey
  • Obey, even as an adult. Seen often
  • Repay the sacrifice. Seen often
  • Never an old-age home. Seen often
  • Keep family problems inside the house. Therapy is "for mad people". Roughly 85% of Indians with a mental health condition get no treatment. Survey
  • Property. 64% say sons and daughters should inherit equally. Whether daughters actually receive their share, or are asked to sign it away, we could not verify. Not sure
L11 · The new "log": friends and the feed
  • Look successful by 25. Seen often
  • Show the trip, the body, the relationship. Seen often
  • Hustle. A side income, a startup, a personal brand. Seen often
  • Have an opinion on everything, today. Seen often
  • Be "chill" about things that hurt. Seen often
  • How much of the pressure is family and how much is peers? We found no data. For some people the feed is heavier than the family. Not sure
Section A · Age 15 to 18

The marks years

You have the least power you will ever have and the most people checking on you.

A1 · What you will be asked
  • "Kitne percent aaye?"
  • "Science liya na?"
  • "Phone rakh do. Sharma ji ka beta dekho."
  • "Ek baar entrance nikal jaye, phir life set hai."
A2 · What is underneath

For most families, education is the only lift that goes up. Your parents fear you getting stuck, and marks are the only dial they know how to read. Seen often

"Life set hai" after the entrance exam is not true. The questions only change. Nobody is lying to you. They were told the same thing.

A3 · Where you live changes it
MetroCoaching from class 8. Compared with the whole apartment WhatsApp group. Seen often
Small cityThree approved careers. A coaching hub in another city is sold as the only way out. Seen often
VillageStaying in school can be the fight, more so for girls. Seven in ten teenage girls say they want to finish graduation. Survey What daily life is like village to village, we cannot say. Not sure
Abroad"We moved here for you." The sacrifice arrives as an invoice. Not sure This is from accounts we have read, not data.
A4 · What you can do
  1. Report before you are asked. Silence frightens parents more than bad news.
  2. Bring a plan. Course, college, jobs, backup. It may still be refused. It will at least be heard.
  3. Borrow an adult. A teacher, older cousin or mama can say your sentence in a voice your parents trust.
  4. Tell one person on the bad day. One mark sheet is not your life. On the day it feels like it is, do not be alone with it.
A5 · The honest part

At 16 you often cannot win. You do not earn, you cannot leave, and the law treats you as a child. Sometimes the only real move is to get through these years with your mind intact and choose later. That is not giving up. It is waiting until you have a vote.

A true line to keep

"Abhi meri nahi chalti. Hamesha aisa nahi rahega."

Right now I don't get a say. It will not always be this way.

Section B · Age 19 to 24

The package years

The question changes from "how much did you score" to "how much do you earn".

B1 · What you will be asked
  • "Placement hua? Kitna package?"
  • "Government job ki taiyari kar lo. Safe hai."
  • "Itni door kyun jaana hai?" (asked more of daughters)
  • "Padhai ho gayi. Ab rishte dekhein?" (in many homes, to women)
B2 · What is underneath

Asked what worries them most, young Indians put their family's financial security first, ahead of their own health. Survey Your first salary is the safety net arriving.

And the fear is not irrational. Jobs for graduates are short. Your parents are wrong about the cure, not about the disease.

B3 · Where you live changes it
MetroThe hustle script. Rent eats the salary. Loneliness is the bill nobody mentions. Seen often
Small citySarkari naukri is the gold standard, so years go into attempts. Relationships stay secret. Seen often
VillageEarn now. Many men migrate for work. For many women, the wedding arrives before the degree. Seen often
AbroadLoans, visas, and "come back" and "don't come back" from the same house. Not sure
B4 · What you can do
  1. Earn something, early. Any amount of your own changes your standing at home.
  2. Put a date on your gamble. "Two attempts, till June 2028, then this plan B." And mean it.
  3. Tell them about your life in small doses. Surprise is what turns a choice into a scandal.
  4. Find out what the family actually needs from you in rupees. A vague duty is heavier than a number.
B5 · The honest part

Choosing your own road can mean being the family disappointment for years. Some parents come round once it works. Some come round and never say so. Some don't. And your road may fail. Then you will hear "I told you so", and part of it will be fair. That is the price of choosing, and it does not have a discount.

A true line to keep

"Jo rasta main chunta hoon, uska bill bhi mera hai."

The road I choose, I pay for.

Section C · Age 25 to 29

The shaadi years

Every wedding you attend becomes a public hearing on your own.

C1 · What you will be asked
  • "Shaadi kab kar rahe ho? Koi hai kya?"
  • "Umar nikal rahi hai." (women hear it earlier and louder)
  • "Settle ho gaye? Ghar liya?" (men hear this one)
  • "Apni community mein hi dekhna."
C2 · What is underneath

A marriage here joins two families, so everybody feels like a shareholder. Your parents also fear dying while you are alone. That fear is real and it is love.

You are not late. The share of 15 to 34 year olds who are married fell 13 points between 2007 and 2021. Survey

C3 · Where you live changes it
MetroA dating app and a biodata running at once. Living alone questioned less. Seen often
Small cityThe clock rings at 24 or 25 for women. A partner from another caste or religion is the hardest news to carry home. Seen often
VillageMany are already married. The questions are about children, earning, and whether a wife may work. Seen often
Abroad"Find someone from our community." Trips home become rishta camps. Not sure
C4 · What you can do
  1. Know your own answer first. Yes, no, not now, or yes but my way.
  2. Say it plainly, once, in private. Vague answers get you more questions and no peace.
  3. If your partner is not who they expect, go in order. Income. A place to stay. One ally. Then tell them. Two adults can marry across caste or religion under the Special Marriage Act, but it requires a 30-day public notice, which can alert a family that objects. Survey Talk to a lawyer or a support group before you file. Not sure how the notice is handled in your district.
  4. If the script has no page for you, because you are queer or do not want marriage, find your people before any announcement. Telling can wait until you are safe.
C5 · The honest part

Marrying your own choice can cost you your family for years, and in some cases for good. In some families and places it is physically dangerous. Marrying their choice to keep the peace also costs, and that bill is paid every day inside the marriage, by two people. There is no free option here. There is only the one whose price you can live with.

A true line to keep

"Galat shaadi akelepan ka ilaaj nahi hai."

The wrong marriage is no cure for being alone.

Section D · Age 30 to 34

The "ab toh" years

"Ab toh kar lo." At least do it now. It fits marriage, baby, flat and second baby.

D1 · What you will be asked
  • "Good news kab de rahe ho?"
  • "Ghar kab le rahe ho?"
  • "Mummy-papa ko paas bula lo." Or: "Wapas aa jao."
  • If single: "Sab theek toh hai na?"
D2 · What is underneath

The roles are flipping. Your parents are ageing and you are becoming the safety net. Sons are named for this duty. Daughters often do the caring without the credit. Survey

On children, a 2026 UN survey found young Indians delay family mainly over money and unequal housework, not because they have stopped wanting one. Survey

D3 · Where you live changes it
MetroEMI, school fees, two jobs. Childcare still lands mostly on her. Seen often
Small cityJoint family negotiations: whose kitchen, whose rules. Seen often
VillageYou are already a senior. The talk is of sons, land, and who must leave for work. Not sure
AbroadParents growing old far away. Guilt across two time zones. Not sure
D4 · What you can do
  1. Have the money talk with your parents. What they have, what they will need, health insurance.
  2. On children, two people have a vote. Say so, kindly, and stop explaining.
  3. If you are single, live fully now. Do not keep your life in a waiting room.
  4. Stop the chain going down. Do not ask your 22-year-old cousin about her package.
D5 · The honest part

You cannot be the perfect child, partner, parent and employee in the same decade. Someone will be let down, and some days it will be the person you love most. Caring for ageing parents is heavy, and no reply on this page makes it lighter. Decide who you will let down, on purpose, instead of letting everyone down by accident.

A true line to keep

"Main sabko khush nahi rakh sakta. Koshish band."

I cannot keep everyone happy. I have stopped trying.

Part 4 · Works at any age

What you can actually do

Non-violent does not mean painless. Some of this will hurt, and some people will stay upset.

W1 · Sort everything into three piles

Not up for vote

Your safety, your body, who you marry, whether you have children, your dignity.

Open to a deal

Timing, city, the size of the wedding, how often you visit, which rituals you keep.

Give freely

The kurta at Diwali, the family photo, the second helping. Give these because you love them. If you give them as payment for freedom, you have started an account.

W2 · What tends to help Seen often
  1. Your own income. The least romantic item and the most reliable.
  2. One true sentence, said plainly, once, in private. Then stop arguing it.
  3. One ally in the family. Let them carry the message where you cannot.
  4. Distance. Moving cities often changes more than any conversation.
  5. Time, in years. Many families adjust to a fact they refused as an idea.
  6. Keep showing up. Festivals, hospital visits, phone calls. Disagreeing and disappearing are different things.
  7. Help for yourself. A counsellor or a friend who listens. You cannot change them. You can be less alone while they stay the same.
W3 · What usually does not work Seen often
  • Sweet talk. It ends the conversation. It does not change the belief.
  • Winning with facts. Nobody was argued into this and nobody gets argued out. That includes the statistics on this page.
  • Ultimatums you will not carry out.
  • Lying to keep the peace. It works for a while. The bill arrives later, with interest.
  • Waiting for approval before you live. Some approvals never come.
  • Humiliating an elder in public. You win the room and lose the person.
W4 · What it can cost
  • Silence, for months or years.
  • Money, a room, or an inheritance withdrawn.
  • Being left out of weddings and funerals.
  • Being talked about by people you grew up with.
  • A guilt that shrinks but may never fully leave.
  • And the cost of not choosing: resentment, and a life arranged for four people who will not be there at the end of it.

Not sure We found no data on how often families come round, or how long it takes. Anyone who tells you "they always accept in the end" is guessing.

When it is not pressure any more

Everything above is for nagging, comparison and guilt. It is not for being hit, locked in, threatened, having your documents taken, or being forced into a marriage. That is harm. Tell someone outside the house and call for help. If the pressure has brought you to thoughts of ending your life, please call too.

Part 5 · The controversial page

Things nobody says out loud

Some of these will annoy your parents. Some will annoy you.

  1. Your parents are right about some of it

    Savings, health, showing up for family. Old advice is not automatically a cage.

  2. You may be the one who is wrong

    Wanting something badly does not make it wise. Some of the choices you defend at 24 you will wince at by 34. Choose anyway, and leave room to say "I was wrong".

  3. Rebellion can be a script too

    Quit, travel, start up, post. If your feed expects it, you have only changed which four people you obey.

  4. Freedom without income is a debate

    You can win every argument at home and still have to ask for rent.

  5. Honesty is cheaper for some people

    A salaried man in a metro can say things that would cost a 19-year-old woman in a small town her education or her safety. Do not preach courage to someone whose price is higher than yours.

  6. We are more modern in surveys than in weddings

    Most young Indians say caste should not matter in marriage. Almost all still marry inside it. Saying it costs nothing. Doing it can cost a family.

  7. Sons and daughters are squeezed by one machine

    She is told what she may not do. He is told what he must provide. Women agree with the "wife must obey" line almost as often as men do. It is everybody against an old rulebook.

  8. Where you were born sets the difficulty

    About a third of people in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka fully agree a wife must always obey. In Uttar Pradesh and Bihar it is about four in five. Survey Do not judge someone's choices until you know what level they are playing on.

  9. Love that is counted becomes debt

    "Humne tumhare liye kitna kiya" is an invoice. So is "I gave up my dreams for them." Both sides keep accounts, and both are poorer for it. What your parents gave cannot be repaid, and it was never meant to be. It can only be passed on.

  10. Some of it will not get better

    Some parents will die still disappointed. Some children will die still unforgiven. Nobody is the villain, and people get hurt anyway. You can do everything on this page gently and correctly, and still lose someone.

Part 6 · Declaration of ignorance

What this page does not know

Listed plainly, so you can weigh everything above.

  • Whether any of the advice works. There is no study behind Part 4. It is common experience, written down.
  • How often families come round, and after how long.
  • Villages. The main youth survey we used interviewed 4,500 urban and 1,500 rural young people. Most Indians live in villages. Our village notes are the weakest part of this page.
  • The North-East, Kashmir, Ladakh and the islands. The surveys cover the 18 biggest states or skip some regions. We know very little here.
  • Life as a Dalit, Adivasi or religious-minority young person. The pressures are not the same, and we have not described them properly.
  • Queer and trans lives. Mentioned, not understood.
  • Disability. Not covered at all. Expectations of disabled young people are a separate and serious subject.
  • Indians abroad. Every "Abroad" line is from accounts we have read, not data.
  • How old the numbers are. The gender-attitudes survey is from 2019-20. The youth survey is from 2021. The caste and marriage data is from 2011-12. Attitudes may have moved.
  • What people really think. Surveys record what people say to an interviewer. That is not always what they do.
  • The law. We are not lawyers. Laws and court rulings change. Check before you act.
  • Your family. You know it. We don't.

Part 7 · To be signed by the applicant

Alone, and not alone

You came into this alone and you will leave it alone. In between, a few people will walk with you. Some because they chose to, some because they were born next to you. None of them owes you agreement, and you do not owe them your life. What you can give each other is company on the road, and that is not a small thing. Do not weigh it. Do not keep score. Just don't waste it.
