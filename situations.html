Part 1 · Instructions

Read this first

Tags mean the same as on Form A: Survey a published source, Seen often familiar but unmeasured, Not sure we don't know.

  1. Every room has its own four people

    At home it is relatives. In the hostel it is seniors. At work it is the boss and the team. In a rented flat it is the landlord and the society secretary. The faces change. The feeling of being watched does not. Seen often

  2. Moving changes the rules, not the game

    You can leave a village for a city and trade "what will the neighbours say" for "what will LinkedIn say". Distance helps. It does not cure.

  3. You will carry two selves

    One for home, one for away. Switching between them on the train is tiring, and it is normal. Almost three in ten Indians live somewhere other than where they last lived. Survey

  4. Leaving is judged. So is coming back.

    Go, and you have abandoned your people. Return, and you have failed. Since both are judged, neither judgement is a reliable guide.

Part 7 · Statements from the other side

How it looks from their chair

Not sure These are imagined, to help you see the other side. They are not real quotes from real people.

A parent

"I had less than you and I was more afraid than you. Nobody asked me what I wanted, so I do not know how to ask you. When I push you, I am trying to buy you a floor I never had. I can see it hurts you. I do not know another way to love you that also keeps you safe."

The aunty who asks

"I ask about your marriage because it is the only question I was taught. Nobody asks me anything any more. If I do not ask about you, what do I have to say at a wedding?"

The sibling who stayed

"You left, and everyone talks about your courage. I stayed. I take Papa to the doctor. Nobody calls that courage. When you visit and tell me to live my own life, remember who made it possible for you to live yours."

The manager

"I was made to stay till ten when I was your age, and I was told it was dedication. I am passing on what was done to me. I have not yet asked myself why."

Part 8 · Declaration of ignorance

What this page does not know

  • Informal and gig work. Most Indians work without a contract. This page says almost nothing useful about that life.
  • Village life from the inside. Written from the outside, and it shows.
  • How many households are joint. Sources disagree, and "joint" is defined differently in each.
  • Renting discrimination. Widely reported. We found no reliable count.
  • Community councils and village bodies. Their power over young people's choices varies enormously by region. We cannot map it.
  • Seasonal and distress migration, where a whole family moves for work. Not covered.
  • Whether "what helps" helps. It is common experience, not tested advice.

Part 9 · Closing

Wherever you are

You will change rooms many times: hostel, office, rented flat, in-laws' house, perhaps back to the room you grew up in. In each one a new set of people will have opinions about you. None of them travels with you to the next room. You do. So do the few people who would know you in any of them. Keep those, and do not count what they cost you.

If a room is not safe

Ragging, harassment at work, being locked in or threatened anywhere is harm, not pressure. Tell someone outside that room.
