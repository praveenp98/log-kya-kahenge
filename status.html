The status games we all play

"Aukaat" means your standing: where you rank in other people's eyes. Most of what Form A calls expectations are moves in a game for it. Here are 28 of those games, how each one is scored, and who pays. You are in several. So are we.

Part 1 · Instructions

How status works

Tags mean the same as on Form A: Survey a published source, Seen often familiar but unmeasured, Not sure we don't know.

  1. Everybody plays. It is human, not Indian.

    Every society ranks its members. The writer Will Storr sorts the games into three kinds: success (what you achieved or own), virtue (how good, pure or dutiful you are) and dominance (who you can push around). India plays all three, with its own tokens.

  2. Here, status buys real things

    How the police speak to you. Whether the landlord says yes. How fast the clerk moves your file. How your daughter is treated in her in-laws' home. So chasing status is often protection, not vanity. Seen often

  3. A rank needs someone below it

    You cannot be "well settled" unless someone else is not. Some games only produce a winner by producing a loser. Those are the ones worth leaving.

  4. The tokens change. The game does not.

    Land became a government job, which became an engineering seat, which became a US visa, which became followers. Your grandparents played too. Seen often

  5. Nobody carries the scoreboard out

    You arrive without a rank and you leave without one. In between, the people worth keeping are the ones who would sit with you at any rank. You cannot measure them, and the moment you try, you are back in a game.

Part 3 · Self-inspection

Signs you are in a game right now

  • You mentioned it without being asked.
  • You felt a small pleasure when someone turned out to have less.
  • You felt smaller the moment someone turned out to have more.
  • You checked who was watching.
  • You could not enjoy it until it had been seen.
  • There is one specific relative you would hate to find out.
  • You explained why you don't care about it, at length.

Part 4 · Options

What you can actually do

Not "stop caring what people think". Nobody has ever managed that.

S1 · Moves
  1. Accept that you cannot quit every game. People who say they don't play are in game 18.
  2. Choose games with a useful by-product. Getting good at a craft leaves you with the craft. A bigger wedding leaves you with a loan.
  3. Leave the games that need someone beneath you. Caste, skin, son, being served. Nothing you win there was earned.
  4. Give status away. Thank the person who cannot do anything for you. Give credit by name. Ask the quietest person what they think.
  5. Stop collecting tokens for people you don't even like. Name the four people. Ask whether their opinion is worth the EMI.
  6. Keep one room with no scoreboard. A friendship, a family dinner, a hobby you are bad at. Don't measure the people in it.
S2 · The honest part

Stepping out has a price. A small wedding can lower your family's standing for real, and they may not forgive you for it. Refusing to share your salary can read as arrogance. Skipping the "right" school can cost your child real doors.

And telling someone with very little status to stop caring about it is a luxury opinion. For a family one generation out of poverty, the house, the degree and the wedding are proof that they got out. Be slow to laugh at that.

Not sure We have no evidence that people who opt out end up happier. Some do. Some just end up lonely in a different way.

Part 5 · Declaration of ignorance

What this page does not know

  • Almost none of the 28 games has a number behind it. They are familiar patterns, described by people who play them.
  • Which games matter most where. A village in Odisha, a chawl in Mumbai and a gated society in Gurugram do not use the same scoreboard.
  • Life at the bottom of the rankings. This page is written from somewhere in the middle. Someone ranked low by caste or class would write a harsher one.
  • Whether the three-kinds idea fits India well. It is one writer's framework, not settled science.
  • Whether naming a game weakens it. We hope so. We have no proof.
  • The games we missed. There are certainly more than 28.

Part 6 · Closing balance

Net worth

Add up every token: the marks, the tag, the package, the flat, the wedding, the followers. Now ask who would still sit with you if the total were zero. That short list is the only asset on this form that is real, and it is the one thing here you must not count.
